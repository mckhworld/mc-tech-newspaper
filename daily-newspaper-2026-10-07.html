The article argues that building a simple baseline model, such as a majority-class guesser or plain logistic regression, is essential before deploying complex algorithms to establish a meaningful performance floor. By testing six public datasets, the author demonstrates that without these cheap baselines, expensive hyperparameter tuning often appears more valuable than it actually is, as the gains are frequently negligible or even negative. This practice matters because it prevents practitioners from mistaking statistical noise for genuine model improvement and forces a critical evaluation of whether complex models truly outperform simpler, interpretable alternatives.

Why a Slow HTTP Call Could Delay Other Kong Requests

https://hackernoon.com/why-a-slow-http-call-could-delay-other-kong-requests📅 2026-10-07 15:43 UTC

A slow HTTP call in a Kong API gateway plugin caused significant delays for unrelated requests because the use of blocking LuaSocket operations stalled the single-threaded NGINX worker. Replacing this with OpenResty cosockets via lua-resty-http allowed the worker to yield during network waits, reducing request times from over a minute to milliseconds and preventing one dependency from impacting overall gateway performance.
